# Interaction feedback

## Current scope

Parks removed interface sounds on November 11, 2026. Launcher buttons, toggles, scrolling and completed operations are silent. The sound settings, preview controls, synthesis engine and delegated audio listeners are removed. Loading settings discards the retired `interactionSounds` field, including previously enabled preferences. Minecraft game audio is outside this change.

## Visual feedback

`useToggleFeedback` retains a short thumb compression on confirmed state changes, separate from state-owned translation. Initial rendering does not squash. Rapid reversals continue from the visible shape. Reduced motion, Show Animations off, Ultra Performance and hidden documents suppress it. Keep the existing configurable button click-pop outside main navigation. Main navigation uses native button `:active` to hold centered compression until release, with a soft scale rebound timed by Button Press Duration. Its inner ceramic face scales separately from the outer 720ms slide track, so pressing cannot scale the route offset or lift the highlight. Pointer-down and Space also freeze an in-flight slide by preserving its computed transform before canceling the old animation. Release resumes from that same position; a newly selected route supersedes the queued resume. Pointer cancellation, focus loss, window blur and unmount clean up the hold. Native click still owns navigation; canceled/outside releases do not select a destination. Motion preferences and zero press duration suppress compression.

## Home Play

Launch and download activity no longer turns off the global animation flag, sidebar motion or animated accent. These follow the user's existing animation/performance preferences; the accent worker still pauses while the document is hidden. Operation guards remain independent of visual feedback.

Home's `PixelPlayButton` owns one clipped decorative fill and duplicate, accessibility-hidden label/icon. A bounded animation-frame loop advances a circular threshold across fixed 10px noise cells, joining adjacent cells into one clip path; no React renders or DOM measurements occur per frame. The 1250ms entrance and 850ms withdrawal use smoothstep easing, reverse from the current progress, and stop at their endpoints. Fresh pointer entry sets the origin; keyboard focus uses the center. Resize rebuilds geometry, and listeners/observers/frames are cleaned up on unmount.

The hover transform grows the button by 2.5%, independently of the shared click-pop's scale/translate longhands. The actual button retains its accessible name, native disabled state, focus outline and original launch callback. Filled colors use `--text` (with the existing theme-ink/fallback convention because OLED does not define that token) and `--page`; the resting accent remains solid under wallpaper/glass. Reduced motion, disabled animations and Ultra Performance snap the fill without scaling. Disabled/busy and hidden-document states stop and clear it. No other Play button or sidebar treatment inherits this effect.

## Verification

`tests/sidebar-press-ui.mjs` checks the real sidebar with intercepted native commands: centered compression on both the first and lower rows, a hold longer than the configured pop duration, interrupted slide holds on every one of the seven routes, release-only navigation, outside release/resume, quick reversals, Space activation, compact Light layout, reduced motion, animations off, Ultra Performance and zero press duration. The existing spring continues on the outer track; screenshot comparisons preserve the approved ceramic frame. Native macOS rendering was not exercised by this Windows browser check.

`useLauncherActivity` maintains one subscription per launch/process event, plus non-overlapping native snapshots while the document is visible (600ms during activity, 1200ms in Ultra Performance, otherwise 3 seconds). Focus/visibility return also refreshes. Snapshots include terminal states and process liveness, so missed completion, error, cancellation or exit events cannot leave the UI indefinitely busy. New events, local state changes and unacknowledged launch requests take priority over stale snapshots. Failed queries preserve the last known state. Repeated snapshots do not replay notifications or revive dismissed completion UI. Late event registrations clean themselves up after unmount. Idle applies immediately; completion cleanup is scoped to the matching state/instance so it cannot reset a new launch.

`tests/launch-recovery-ui.mjs` reproduces the original launch-time global animation shutdown and checks navigation/selection while busy, dropped terminal/process events, stale responses, a new launch after idle, pending commands, transient query failures, Strict Mode subscription cleanup and saved motion/performance preferences. Native commands are intercepted rather than starting Minecraft.

Check that previously enabled sound preferences cannot create an AudioContext or restore the removed settings after reload, and that toggles still change state and animate. Run the repository typecheck, production build and locked Cargo check. The November 7 sound experiment and prior test results are historical in `UI_CHANGE_HISTORY.md`, not current requirements.

`tests/pixel-play-ui.mjs` exercises the actual Home component against intercepted native commands: pointer origins, partial/full/reset fill, quick reversal, stable selector geometry, keyboard and motion settings, theme/glass, resize, and immediate launch of the selected instance. It does not start a real Minecraft process.

`tests/downloads-ui.mjs` verifies active/completed missing-artwork containment and noninterference with hit testing, then exercises Cancel, navigation, Clear Completed and history persistence through isolated native/browser fixtures. It does not clear the user's actual history or cancel a real download.

## Downloads history

Content installations use the native `ContentQueue` in `src-tauri/src/content_downloads.rs`, with UUID task identity, FIFO execution and per-task cancellation. The existing per-file download workers still run within the active task; separate content tasks stay serialized to avoid simultaneous writes to shared dependencies. `content-downloads-changed` publishes revisioned snapshots, and `get_content_downloads` recovers missed events through a bounded non-overlapping poll. Progress events are limited to roughly ten per second; task transitions publish immediately. Game launch events remain independent. Active and pending duplicates for the same instance/project/category are rejected as duplicate work; their original record is returned.

`useContentDownloads` keeps the accepted snapshot across navigation, ignores older revisions and recovers on focus/reload. Successful task records are synchronously added to the existing `bloom-completed-downloads` storage before recording the native session/revision in `bloom-content-queue-observed-v1`. That checkpoint prevents duplicate terminal notifications and cleared history returning after a webview reload. History retains the latest 50 tasks; native snapshots retain 100 terminal records plus up to 100 pending tasks. The native queue survives webview reloads within the running process; quitting/restarting the entire application does not resume unfinished downloads. Failed/cancelled records remain dismissible for the current process. Catalog pending/installed state reads the same queue, and errors also enter launcher logs.

`DownloadHistory` retains only presentation snapshots for removed rows. Clear Completed invokes the existing parent clear immediately; retained rows become inert and accessibility-hidden. The revised exit falls 28px downward and scales to 90% over 720ms with S-curve easing. A separate 540ms eased fade/2px blur begins at 180ms. Height/gap collapse starts at 480ms and eases over 560ms so readable rows depart before their space closes; presentation wrappers do not clip the falling card. Stagger runs from the bottom row upward, with an 80ms step compressed proportionally for long lists so the total spread never exceeds 400ms. Five rows finish exiting in 1360ms; any supported list finishes within 1440ms. The empty state fades in over 320ms. Finished animation styles stay applied until React removes the row, preventing an end-frame flash. Incoming history entries supersede matching exit snapshots and never get removed by animation completion.

The existing flat download rows gain a 1px hover lift and neutral border/fill response; the clear action tilts its trash icon slightly and uses the existing danger-ink token on hover/focus. Empty-list space is reserved to prevent the footer jumping when the empty state appears. Reduced motion, disabled animations, Ultra Performance, hidden documents and unmount skip or clean up decorative exits. The Downloads browser regression verifies clearing while an exit is paused, arrival of another completion, keyboard clearing and immediate removal under motion overrides.

## Account-menu logout Undo

`UndoLogoutButton` owns a five-second, nonpersistent request keyed to the current Minecraft account. Its deadline timer is authoritative; the SVG perimeter stroke measured from the real button’s four corner radii only displays progress and never triggers sign-out through an animation event. Undo, hiding/closing the menu, switching accounts, hidden documents and unmount clear both timers. The deadline rechecks current visibility, account and disabled state before invoking the existing `sign_out_minecraft` callback. The parent rejects duplicate native requests and retains its account refresh and error handling.

The button changes Log out → Undo → Signing out in the same footprint. `ResizeObserver` keeps the ring on the real button perimeter, including the compact sidebar. Progress updates locally every 100ms; reduced/off/Ultra renders whole-second steps and removes transitions. The account drawer's motion and other actions are unchanged. `tests/logout-undo-ui.mjs` uses an isolated native bridge and controlled time to cover cancellation, deadline commit, keyboard, failures, duplicate suppression and motion/compact behavior; it never signs out a real account.

The logout footer sits inside the borderless account menu, keeping its icon/label alignment and font family, with all four corners rounded to 13px. At Parks's explicit request it uses the account dock's ceramic bevel tokens and 160-degree shading on a nearly black neutral face, casting a soft shadow upward over the dropdown. Its label and icon carry more weight. The red danger-ink perimeter follows those corners and solely represents remaining Undo time. Hover remains neutral and Light theme adapts the face and text. These surface changes leave the five-second deadline and cancellation behavior unchanged.


## Ceramic surfaces

The account dropdown uses an opaque, borderless panel with inset action rows and a contained logout control. The established clip reveal and account-dock movement remain unchanged; the outer shadow sits outside the clipped panel.

`src/components/ceramic-actions.css` supplies shared material to instance/folder/content actions and Back states, account/setup actions, Settings including Clear cache/Change, on toggles and Enabled, photo/preview/comparison actions and Cancel, cosmetics, sharing, social, utilities and Auto Tune. Existing colors, geometry, handlers, disabled states and toggle feedback remain authoritative; Home Play retains its pixel material. Future matching controls can use `.ceramic-action` rather than copying a bevel.

The final correction limits the faint inset highlight to the upper-left edge with negative spread and removes surrounding inner shading. Small utility controls have a dull neutral face and a modest hover/focus lift, fading in and out over 200ms. Reduced motion, animations off and Ultra do not add this fade; root Ultra tokens remove both normal and compact depth. Explicit material shadows override the general button reset.
